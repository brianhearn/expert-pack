# Energy Dashboard and kWh Sensors

The Energy Dashboard tracks grid, solar, return-to-grid, battery, gas, water, and individual devices using long-term statistics. It requires cumulative energy sensors in kWh with `state_class: total_increasing` — power in watts is not enough.

## What the Energy Dashboard Shows

The Energy Dashboard (Settings → Energy) tracks:

- **Grid consumption** — energy drawn from the utility grid (kWh)
- **Solar production** — energy generated by solar panels (kWh)
- **Return to grid** — excess solar sent back to the grid (kWh)
- **Battery storage** — charge into and discharge from battery storage (kWh)
- **Gas consumption** — natural gas or propane usage (m³ or ft³)
- **Water consumption** — water usage (L or gal)
- **Individual devices** — specific appliances (EV charger, washer, HVAC, etc.)

All data feeds into long-term statistics that persist in the HA database independently of state history. You can view daily/weekly/monthly/yearly summaries even after purging the recorder database.

## The Critical Sensor Requirement

This is the most common stumbling block. The Energy Dashboard **requires sensors that:**

1. Report in **kWh** (kilowatt-hours) — energy, not power
2. Have `state_class: total_increasing` set correctly
3. **Never decrease** (except on device reboot/counter reset, which `total_increasing` handles)

**Power (watts, W) ≠ Energy (kilowatt-hours, kWh)**

| Concept | Unit | What it measures | Analogy |
|---------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Power | Watts (W) | Rate of consumption right now | Speed (km/h) |
| Energy | Kilowatt-hours (kWh) | Total consumption over time | Distance (km) |

The dashboard wants the odometer reading, not the speedometer. Many integrations provide both; some only provide power.

**What `state_class: total_increasing` does:**
- Tells HA this sensor is a cumulative counter that always goes up
- HA tracks the start value and calculates differences for statistics
- When the sensor resets to zero (device reboot, counter overflow), HA detects the drop and continues accumulating from the new value — no data loss

**Checking your sensor's state_class:**
Developer Tools → States → search for your sensor → expand attributes → look for `state_class: total_increasing`

If it says `measurement` instead of `total_increasing`, or if it's missing entirely, the sensor won't work with the Energy Dashboard.
